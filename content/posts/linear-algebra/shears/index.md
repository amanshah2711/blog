## Elementary Shears
Given a linear functional $\mathbf{w}^{H}$ and a vector $\mathbf{v}$ contained inside the hyperplane through the origin, $\mathbf{w}^{H}\mathbf{x} = 0$, we can define an elementary shear as $\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{v}\mathbf{w}^{H}$. The geometric idea of a simple shear is that a plane remains fixed and all vectors not contained in this plane get “pushed” in some direction. The best example of this phenomena is a deck of cards  on a table viewed from its side, pushed so the once straight edge perpendicular to the table now is a slanted edge.

## Connection to Area (Cavalieri’s Principle)

Cavalieri’s principle is an idea that is widespread and used by others such as Lebesgue. It states the simple idea that if you have a deck of cards, and shear the deck of cards as described earlier the amount of area taken up by the deck of cards does not change.
