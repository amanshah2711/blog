## Introduction
 
Linear algebra as the name implies is a subject that involves algebra. Linear algebra is intimately tied to geometry however, does exist independently of it. There is a delicate interplay between geometry and algebra and often times there is the ability to prove a result in algebra by taking cues from geometry and vice versa. In this spirit we hope to lay out the algebraic notions needed. Algebraic notions have the benefit of being simple in definition, though illusory in their raison d’être. This note ought to be read in chunks: the first three headers at baseline, and the rest as they arise in other notes.
